## Writing lanes: desk, writing, proofreading desk

A piece of writing (a post, a card set, a script, a legal document) moves the way it does in a newsroom, and a brief for a writing lane hands it over in that order.

At the desk the lane settles who the one reader is, the question that reader brought, and the conclusion the piece has to land on. Then it gathers the material: the statute, judgment or form in its original words, and a passed piece of the same kind. Collecting public material and the first cold read as a stranger can go to the pirate-ship workers; client material never does. The task file says only who the reader is, what the material is and how long the piece runs. It doesn't pile up instructions about how to write sentences. That's the digest's job (`DORI_HOME/독후감/소화본/<kind>.md`), and two such instructions can contradict each other in a way no writer can satisfy. When a task file and the digest or the owner's words disagree, fix the task file.

For the writing itself the lane runs `bun tools/writer_request.ts build` from the dori checkout, with this harness's `DORI_HOME`; the build puts the digest first on its own and recalls the 독후감 passages the task calls for. Before each `send` the lane reads the recalled passages the request's `.count` file lists, and if one holds a person's real name or any other identifier (registration, phone or case number, address) it builds again with `--no-recall` and its own `--cases PATH:START-END` lines, leaving that passage out. The skeleton comes first: `bun tools/writer_request.ts build --kind <kind> --model hydrogen --label skeleton --lane-dir <lane dir> --task <file>`, where the task file asks Hydrogen for the title and the headings only, each with the reader question it answers, and the lane reports it to you with `--status waiting --evidence <skeleton file>` and stops. You read it against the reader's questions and the owner's words and answer with `{{dori}} send <key> --text '...'`, saying which heading answers which question, which question has none and what to change ("OK" alone isn't a reading). The lane saves that answer as `<lane dir>/requests/skeleton_answer.txt` and builds the body with `--model argon --task <skeleton file> --task <lane dir>/requests/skeleton_answer.txt --task <body task file>`, in that order. A skeleton never goes to the human.

The proofreading desk is `review.py` in sanjai-agent's `scripts/delivery_review`, given the piece and the task file with `--task`; it reads its own earlier rounds and the digest by itself. Only the file it copies into `나갈판/` after saying `나가도 됩니다` goes to the human, byte for byte. If it says `아직 안 됩니다`, the lane sends its `fix_sheet.txt` through `build --sheet`, takes back just the flagged sentences with `fill`, and puts them in place. Nobody rewrites the whole piece for a flagged sentence, and a version the desk flagged is never delivered.

When the owner sends a piece back, the lane fixes the spot and records the before and after with `writer_request pair`. Then the digest for that kind gets rewritten: the passage that let the mistake through is rewritten, not appended to, and the first line gets the new time in exactly the form `소화: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`. A digest without that line stops every build of its kind.

An order to write a 독후감 or to do 파쿠리 isn't done when the notes are written. The lane says which passage of the digest the study changes and how, runs one real job with the changed digest, and sets the before and after side by side. If it got better the change stays; if not, it's reverted. Only then is the order finished.
